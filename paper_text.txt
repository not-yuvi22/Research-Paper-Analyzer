A Lightweight SAR Ship Detector Using
End-to-End Image Preprocessing Network and
Channel Feature Guided Spatial Pyramid Pooling
Chuxuan Chen , Student Member, IEEE, Yimin Zhang , Ronglin Hu, and Yongtao Yu , Senior Member, IEEE
Abstract— Recently, in the field of synthetic aperture radar
(SAR) ship detection, deep-learning-based methods have made
significant strides in terms of detection accuracy and speed.
However, small-scale targets and complex backgrounds remain
a formidable obstacle to SAR ship detection. To overcome the
aforementioned challenges, this letter proposes LiteSAR-Net,
a lightweight SAR ship detector, to enhance ship detection
capabilities in SAR imagery. In detail, an end-to-end image
preprocessing network (E2IPNet) is proposed to strengthen
context information and expand the network’s effective receptive
field. In addition, to prevent the dilution of semantic information,
the channel feature guided spatial pyramid pooling (CFGSPP) is
proposed, which can adjust the parameters adaptively based on
interchannel information. The proposed LiteSAR-Net achieved
an average precision (AP) of 98.61% on the SAR ship detection
dataset (SSDD) and 93.33% on the high-resolution SAR images
dataset (HRSID), with a parameter of only 5.247 M, outper-
formed many state-of-the-art (SOTA) detectors. Our code can be
found at https://github.com/ZYMCCX/LiteSAR-Net.
Index Terms— Deep learning, lightweight, object detection,
ship detection, synthetic aperture radar (SAR).

II. PROPOSED METHOD
A. Overall Architecture
Considering the characteristics of ships in SAR images,
a lightweight SAR ship detector, LiteSAR-Net, is proposed in
this letter, and its overall architecture is depicted in Fig. 1.
The proposed LiteSAR-Net consists of four parts: image
preprocessing, backbone, neck, and prediction head. First,
in the image preprocessing component, the input image will
be preprocessed by the proposed E2IPNet to enhance the
context information of the image for better detection of ships
under complex backgrounds in SAR images. Subsequently,
the feature maps processed by the E2IPNet will be fed
into the backbone consisting of cross-stage partial network
(CSPNet) and CFGSPP, which will be able to extract a set
of feature maps for detecting ships at different scales. After
that, for better multiscale ship detection in SAR images, neck,
YOLOX-tiny’s path aggregation feature pyramid network
(PAFPN), will process the extracted feature map

B. End-to-End Image Preprocessing Network
Due to noise such as land structures, clutter, and coastlines
in SAR images, current SAR ship detectors have difficulty
locating ships in complex backgrounds. To improve ship
detection with complex scenes, this letter proposes a U-shaped
image preprocessing network, E2IPNet, which can enhance the
ship’s outline features and context information. The workflow
of the designed E2IPNet is shown in Fig. 2.
At first, to extract ship features, the input I ∈R3×W×H will
be reshaped into a F ∈R48×W/4×H/4 by two proposed asym-
metric large kernel balance blocks (ALKB-Blocks), which are
capable of efficiently fusing context and local information.
Subsequently, this feature map will be refined by convolutional
block attention module (CBAM) [15]. Following that, to fuse
the feature map, we use the ALKB-Block in conjunction
with upsampling to effectively upscale the refined feature
maps. Besides, to further enhance the context and position
information of ships, we add the upscaled feature maps with
the previous results directly. Finally, after two such operations,
a processed I ′ ∈R3×W×H is obtained to be utilized as an input
for the following network. The details about ALBK-Block and
CBAM are shown below.
1) Asymmetric Large Kernel Balance Block: Enhanced con-
text information can improve the detection of small ships
and ships with complex backgrounds. To better merge context
information with local information and to expand the effective
sensing field [16], a new convolution block ALKB-Block is
proposed, and its structure is presented in Fig. 2.
Large kernel convolution can effectively expand the ERF
and extract more context information. However, large kernel
convolution is computationally intensive and results in the loss
of local information. Therefore, to balance the global and local
information, we utilize a set of asymmetric convolution blocks
with kernel sizes of 13 × 3 and 3 × 13 to process the feature
maps. This allows for less computing complexity and a more
optimal distribution of local and global knowledge. In addition,
we also add a 3 × 3 convolution block to further balance the
context information. Finally, the output of the ALKB-Block is
the sum of the three convolutional outputs. The workflow of
the designed ALKB-Block can be calculated as

III. EXPERIMENTS
A. Datasets and Implementation Details
1) Datasets Description: Two public challenging datasets,
the SSDD and the HRSID, are introduced to validate the
proposed LiteSAR-Net. SSDD is one of the most widely uti-
lized SSDDs. This dataset contains 1160 images with varying
resolutions, of which 928 images are used for training and the
remaining 232 images are used for testing. HRSID is a high-
resolution dataset. The HRSID dataset contains 5604 images
with a resolution of 800 × 800, of which 3642 are used for
training and 1962 for testing.
2) Training Details:
In this letter, stochastic gradient
descent (SGD) is used as the optimization algorithm, with the
initial learning rate set to 0.01, the momentum set to 0.937, and
the weight decay set to 0.0005. We set the batch size to 16 and
trained 300 epochs. The input image sizes are 416 × 416 in
SSDD and 800 × 800 in HRSID. All experiments in this letter
are conducted on an Nvidia RTX 3090 GPU.
3) Evaluation Metrics: To evaluate the performance of the
proposed LiteSAR-Net, average precision (AP), F1, frames
per second (fps), and parameters are introduced. Whereas
AP and F1 are used to evaluate detection accuracy, fps, and
parameters are utilized to evaluate computational complexity
and efficiency.
B. Ablation Experiments
1) Effectiveness of Each Proposed Module: In this section,
experiments are conducted on SSDD and HRSID to verify
the efficacy of the proposed E2IPNet and CFGSPP. As is
shown in Table I, the AP of the baseline model on the SSDD
and HRSID datasets is 95.08% and 90.65%, respectively,
while the F1 is 0.95 and 0.89, with a parameter of 5.033 M.
Compared to the baseline model, the LiteSAR-Net proposed
in this letter achieves an AP for 98.61% (+3.53%) and
93.33% (+2.68%) on SSDD and HRSID, respectively, with a
parameter of 5.247 M (+4.2%). With the introduction of the
designed E2IPNet, the model surpasses by 2.56% and 2.32%,
respectively, in terms of AP metrics on the SSDD and HRSID
datasets. On the F1 metric, the addition of E2IPNet improves
the baseline model by 0.01 on both datasets.

further validate the effectiveness of the proposed LiteSAR-Net.
LiteSAR-Net is compared with both lightweight and classi-
cal SOTA detectors. For lightweight detectors, we include
YOLOv5-s, YOLOX-s [6], RTMDet-s [7], and YOLOv8-s
in our comparison. As for classical SOTA detectors, our
comparison encompasses Faster R-CNN [2], YOLOv7 [4],
and FCOS [5]. Furthermore, we compare LiteSAR-Net with
the latest SAR ship detectors, such as the method proposed
by Yang et al. [10], BL-Net [11], FBUA-Net [12], FL-CSE-
ROIE [13], and CSnNet [14]. The results of these comparisons

IV. CONCLUSION
This letter proposes LiteSAR-Net, an innovative lightweight
SAR ship detector, with the intent of enhancing ship detection
capabilities in SAR imagery. On the one hand, E2IPNet is
designed to expand the network’s effective receptive field
and to improve the context information of ships in SAR
images. CFGSPP, on the other hand, is proposed to reduce
the loss of semantic information and to fuse multiscale
ship features. Experimental results demonstrate that the pro-
posed LiteSAR-Net outperforms other existing SOTA methods
in terms of precision and efficiency. However, horizontal
bounding boxes-based object detection introduces more clutter
regions and is prone to causing false negatives in postprocess-
ing. In our future work, we will attempt to introduce oriented
bounding boxes-based object detectors to tackle this challenge
at its core.
